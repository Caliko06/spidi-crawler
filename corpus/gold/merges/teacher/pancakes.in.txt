DOCUMENT: Easy pancakes


THEME "classic pancake batter"
  s0 Prevent lumps forming by slowly whisking the liquid into the flour.
  s1 If you do get some lumps, whizz it in a food processor.

THEME "What is the best pan to make these pancakes?"
  s2 For crêpes, you can use a non-stick frying pan, if you have one with sloped sides, that's good for sliding them out once cooked.
  s3 A seasoned cast iron pan will work well for both American style and crêpes.
  s4 Avoid stainless steel frying pans, as if you are not familiar cooking in them, the risk of sticking is quite high.

THEME "How much batter should I use per pancake?"
  s5 For crêpes, use a ladle and pour enough into the pan, swirling it so that it just coats the bottom, if you are using a large pan, you can double the recipe.
  s6 For American-style use 2-3 tbsp of batter per pancake.

THEME "My pancakes are not cooking in the middle"
  s7 You may be using too much batter for each pancake.
  s8 Aim for a thin layer on the bottom of the pan to ensure it cooks evenly.

THEME "What should I do if the batter seems too thin/runny?"
  s9 This classic pancake batter is supposed to be thin, like the consistency of single cream.

THEME "Easy pancakes"
  s10 [LEAD] When hot, cook your pancakes for 1 min on each side until golden, using around half a ladleful of batter per pancake.

THEME "Method"


SUBJECT "step 1" (under theme "Method")
  s11 Put 100g plain flour, 2 large eggs, 300ml milk, 1 tbsp sunflower or vegetable oil and a pinch of salt into a bowl or large jug, then whisk to a smooth batter.
