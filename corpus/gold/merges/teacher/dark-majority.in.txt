DOCUMENT: The Dark Majority


THEME "A planet of deep water"
  s0 [ESSENTIAL] Most of the living space on this planet has never seen sunlight.
  s1 [ESSENTIAL] Oceans cover about 71% of Earth's surface, but the surface is the smallest part of the story.
  s2 The average depth is close to 3,700 metres.
  s3 Sunlight gives up long before that.
  s4 By 200 metres there is too little of it for photosynthesis, and by 1,000 metres it is gone completely.
  s5 Everything below that line is the deep sea.
  s6 It holds roughly 90% of the ocean's volume.
  s7 [ESSENTIAL] In other words, the typical place to live on Earth is cold, dark and pressed under kilometres of water.

THEME "Food falls from above"
  s8 Almost nothing grows in the deep.
  s9 Most of its food arrives as marine snow: a slow drift of dead plankton, waste and dust from the sunlit water far above.
  s10 Only about 1% of what the surface produces ever lands on the seafloor.
  s11 Hydrothermal vents, discovered in 1977, run on chemistry instead of sunlight.
  s12 At the vents, water heated by magma emerges at up to 400 °C.
  s13 Bacteria feed on the minerals dissolved in it, and tube worms more than 2 metres long house the bacteria.
  s14 Ultimately, it is a food chain that starts with rock, not sunlight.

THEME "Light made by life"
  s15 Where the sun stops, animals take over the job.
  s16 A survey off the California coast found that 76% of the animals observed in the water column could produce their own light.
  s17 Bioluminescence is not a curiosity.
  s18 It may be the most common form of communication on the planet.
  s19 The light is used to hunt, to hide and to find a mate.
  s20 In the dark, light is language.

THEME "Pressure"
  s21 Pressure rises by one atmosphere every 10 metres.
  s22 At the bottom of the Challenger Deep, nearly 11 kilometres down, it passes 1,000 atmospheres.
  s23 The animals that live there do not resist the pressure so much as ignore it.
  s24 Their bodies are mostly water, which barely compresses, and their proteins are steadied by small molecules that keep them folding correctly.
  s25 The result is an environment that barely changes from one century to the next.

THEME "The Dark Majority"
  s26 [ESSENTIAL] We have barely looked at it.
  s27 [LEAD] Put simply: we cannot protect what we have never seen.

THEME "The map is mostly blank"
  s28 Of our own seafloor, only about a quarter has been mapped at high resolution.
  s29 The rest is known from satellite estimates that blur anything smaller than a few kilometres.
  s30 People first reached the deepest point of the ocean in 1960.
  s31 Nobody went back for 52 years.

THEME "Why it matters"
  s32 The deep sea stores heat and carbon on a scale that shapes the climate of the whole planet.
  s33 It is also being considered for mining before it has been properly surveyed.
  s34 That is why the next decade of exploration matters more than the last century.
